\section{Introduction}

\section{Methodology}

\subsection{Problem Definition and Preliminaries}

\paragraph{Environment and agents.}
We study a partially observable, adversarial multi-robot setting instantiated as a 2-vs-2 naval
capture-the-flag task on a symmetric maritime arena. Two teams, Blue (learner) and Red
(opponent), each control $|B|=2$ autonomous surface vessels. We model the interaction as a
finite-horizon partially observable Markov game
\[
G =
\left(
\mathcal{N},\mathcal{S},\{\mathcal{A}_i\}_{i\in\mathcal{N}},P,
\{r_i\}_{i\in\mathcal{N}},\{\Omega_i\}_{i\in\mathcal{N}},\{O_i\}_{i\in\mathcal{N}},\gamma
\right),
\]
with $\mathcal{N}=B\cup R$.

At each simulator tick $t$, agent $i$ receives $o_{i,t} \sim O_i(\cdot \mid s_t)$ and selects a
macro-action $a_{i,t}\in\mathcal{A}_i$ from a fixed macro vocabulary
$\{\texttt{GO\_TO},\texttt{GRAB\_MINE},\texttt{GET\_FLAG},\texttt{PLACE\_MINE},\texttt{GO\_HOME}\}$,
paired with a target selection. The joint action space is
$\mathrm{MultiDiscrete}([5,50,5,50])$: per agent, a $5$-way macro and a $50$-way target. The
joint action induces $s_{t+1}\sim P(\cdot\mid s_t,a_t)$.

Each team's flag is anchored at its home base; a carrier who returns the enemy flag alive scores
a capture. Mines are stationary hazards placed by an armed agent at its current cell, creating
area-denial and ambush dynamics symmetric across teams.

\paragraph{Strategic poles and strategic demand.}
Red is instantiated from one of two scripted archetypes we call \emph{poles}. Pole A
(opponent \textbf{OP6} with a frozen genome overlay) presents a wide, passive zone defense
($\texttt{defender\_zone\_frac}=0.35$, $\texttt{threat\_radius}=0$); Pole B (opponent
\textbf{OP7}, canonical) presents a tight, reactive defense ($\texttt{defender\_zone\_frac}=0.05$,
$\texttt{threat\_radius}=12$). These are qualitatively different defensive philosophies rather
than two difficulty settings of one behavior, and the pole assignment is resolved through an
asserted seam that verifies the live behavior-tree profile rather than trusting an override flag.

We do not assume these conditions require different Blue behavior; we certify it. A held-out
strategic-demand test measures the win-rate advantage of a GUARD-biased response over a
BREACH-biased response on Pole A, and the reverse contrast on Pole B, gated at
$\mathrm{LCB}_{95}>0$. Both directions pass. This is the precondition for everything that
follows: if one policy were simply better everywhere, compression into a single behavior would
be the correct answer rather than a compression \emph{problem}.

\paragraph{Policies and the crossover criterion.}
Let $\pi_\theta(a\mid o,z)$ denote a policy conditioned on a discrete latent strategy variable
$z\in\{z_0,z_1\}$, and let $V(z,p)$ denote its win rate when deployed with latent $z$ against
pole $p\in\{A,B\}$, estimated over a held-out seed block. We define the \textbf{strategic
crossover criterion}
\[
\Delta_A = V(z_0,A)-V(z_1,A),
\qquad
\Delta_B = V(z_1,B)-V(z_0,B),
\]
with pass condition
\[
\mathrm{LCB}_{95}(\Delta_A)>0
\quad\wedge\quad
\mathrm{LCB}_{95}(\Delta_B)>0,
\]
evaluated by a paired percentile bootstrap over the held-out seeds with
$n_{\mathrm{boot}}=20{,}000$, $\alpha=0.05$, and the seed as the resampling unit.

Crossover is the claim that $z_0$ and $z_1$ are not merely \emph{different} but \emph{differently
good in the direction that matters}: each latent must be the better choice specifically on the
pole it is meant to carry. Every gate in this work uses this identical criterion; no threshold is
adjusted after an outcome is observed.

The binary win indicator is retained as the primary outcome because it is the deployment quantity
used by every crossover gate, but we do not treat a single $64$-seed estimate as a precise
effect-size measurement. Historical one-shot stage gates use their originally frozen $64$-seed
blocks and are reported as such. After the instrument-calibration experiment in
Section~\ref{sec:matched}, all parameter-sharing claims use the frozen matched $128$-seed
reference and within-seed paired differences. Thus the original gates are preserved without
retroactively changing their rules, while the final architecture comparison uses the
higher-powered estimator motivated by the observed sampling variance.

\paragraph{Preregistration and integrity discipline.}
Each stage is governed by a specification frozen and committed to version control \emph{before}
its implementation, with amendments appended rather than edited in place. Sealed evaluation seed
blocks are one-shot: a block is spent once, never re-scored for the same arm. Any tie or reversal
on a gated quantity triggers a mandatory row-level integrity audit, written before its rows are
read, which classifies the result as genuine or as an evaluator defect before any interpretation
is permitted. Experiments terminated for resource reasons are recorded as \textsc{aborted} with
no verdict, explicitly distinguished from \textsc{fail}, and their partially exposed seed blocks
are retired from reuse.

\paragraph{Scope of claims and external-validity boundary.}
The causal claims in the mechanism study are intentionally restricted to the nominal $2$-vs-$2$,
two-pole maritime CTF setting described above. We do not infer from these experiments alone that
the same sharing boundary holds for larger teams, different opponent families, or perturbed
sensing and control. Scaling to $4$-vs-$4$ and $6$-vs-$6$, and deployment perturbations such as
localization noise, motion drift, and control delay, are treated as \emph{external-validity
tests} rather than as evidence used to choose among mechanisms. Until those tests are completed,
conclusions are stated at the level of this environment and these two strategic poles.

\paragraph{Execution device as part of the measurement instrument.}
A prospective CPU--CUDA A/B check on identical seeds showed that floating-point differences can
change simulator trajectories and terminal scores. We therefore treat execution device as part
of the experimental instrument rather than as a mere performance setting. Sealed crossover
evaluations and all matched-seed ladder comparisons are run on the same frozen CUDA path.
Diagnostics collected on CPU are explicitly scoped to the CPU-generated state distribution and
are never used to claim device-invariant behavior.

\subsection{Reference Points: Specialists and Generalist}

\paragraph{Complementary specialists (upper reference).}
Before attempting compression we establish that complementary specialization is achievable at
all. We train two independent policies $\pi_A,\pi_B$ under matched conditions, one per pole, with
no shared parameters and no latent mechanism. Scored under a preregistered protocol on a fresh
$64$-seed block, separate specialists confirm crossover:
\[
\Delta_A = +0.172\;[+0.016,+0.328],
\qquad
\Delta_B = +0.297\;[+0.125,+0.469],
\]
both clearing the gate. Note the narrowness of $\mathrm{LCB}_{95}(\Delta_A)=+0.016$; this margin
becomes important in Section~\ref{sec:matched}.

\paragraph{Single generalist (lower reference).}
As the complementary lower reference we evaluate $\pi_G$, a single policy trained on the mixed
opponent distribution with no latent mechanism ($\texttt{latent\_k}=0$, verified by loading the
checkpoint rather than trusting its recorded configuration). It attains
$V(\pi_G,A)=0.375\;[0.265,0.500]$ and $V(\pi_G,B)=0.500\;[0.375,0.625]$, well below the
specialists on their own poles. The intended hierarchy is therefore
\[
\text{generalist} \;<\; \text{latent specialization} \;\le\; \text{separate specialists},
\]
with the two endpoints measured and the middle term the object of study.

\subsection{Latent-Conditioned Policy Compression}

\paragraph{Architecture.}
The compressed policy $\pi_\theta$ shares a perception trunk (CNN encoder) and an MLP body
across $z_0$ and $z_1$, branching into private per-latent actor capacity and private per-latent
critic heads $V_{z_0},V_{z_1}$. Of its $39$ parameter tensors, $17$ are genuinely shared on the
$z$-conditioned forward path (verified by perturbation testing rather than assumed from parameter
names), $2$ are vestigial, and $20$ are private per latent.

The intended deployment mapping is $z_0\mapsto\text{Pole A}$, $z_1\mapsto\text{Pole B}$. Once
frozen, this assignment is never flipped by any method in this work; only the \emph{target
behavior} supervised into each latent changes across methods.

\paragraph{The behavioral-supervision ladder.}
We evaluate increasingly capable behavioral-supervision treatments, each holding the crossover
criterion as the gate: one-sided latent distillation; paired latent distillation on matched
states; trajectory-guided distillation with private per-latent actor capacity; and
private-critic trajectory distillation. None clears the gate.

\paragraph{Central finding of the behavioral ladder.}
Across the ladder, \emph{behavioral} identity (single-state action agreement with the matched
specialist) and \emph{full-trajectory} identity (aggregate rollout separability from a held-out
discriminator controlled for opponent identity) both become strongly and independently confirmed
as compression sophistication increases. Yet strategic payoff, measured only by the crossover
criterion, never follows:
\[
\underbrace{\text{teacher imitation}}_{\text{established}}
\;\longrightarrow\;
\underbrace{\text{latent/trajectory identity}}_{\text{confirmed}}
\;\longrightarrow\;
\underbrace{\text{strategic payoff}}_{\text{broken}}.
\]
Diversity certified at the level of single-state behavior or whole-trajectory identity is not
sufficient evidence of the payoff-relevant strategic difference the deployment criterion actually
measures. This motivates a change in \emph{what} is supervised rather than a further increase in
\emph{how faithfully} behavior is imitated.

\subsection{Semi-MDP Commitment Structure}

A macro-action, once accepted, is held for a macro-specific number of ticks before the agent may
commit to a new one:
$\texttt{GO\_TO}=4$, $\texttt{GRAB\_MINE}=3$, $\texttt{GET\_FLAG}=4$, $\texttt{PLACE\_MINE}=2$,
$\texttt{GO\_HOME}=4$. Each agent $i$ carries a countdown $c_{i,t}$, and a newly requested macro
is accepted only when the per-agent predicate
\[
d_t^i=\mathbb{I}\!\left[c_{i,t}\le 0\right]
\]
holds. At all other ticks the legal-action mask collapses to the already-committed macro, so the
policy's output is not an executable decision. Because hold lengths differ by macro and agents
commit independently, decision boundaries fall \textbf{asynchronously} across agents.

This reframes the task, for supervision purposes, as a semi-Markov decision process over
commitment decisions rather than a flat per-tick MDP, and it directly motivates the causal
treatment below: the behavioral ladder's supervision was applied without regard to $d_t^i$,
meaning much of its supervised mass fell on ticks that were never executable decisions.

\subsection{Incumbent-Relative Causal Commitment Supervision}
\label{sec:causal-supervision}

Rather than supervising resemblance to a specialist's \emph{behavior}, we supervise toward
specialist interventions with a measured \textbf{causal, incumbent-relative effect on terminal
payoff}, applied only at real commitment boundaries where $d_t^i=1$.

\subsubsection{Causal Estimand}
For a boundary state $s_t$, drawn from a prospectively selected, outcome-blind, stratified sample
of the incumbent's own deployment distribution, and an estimand $e$ identifying which agent(s)
may be intervened on, we define three matched-continuation arms sharing an identical continuation
seed $r_j$:
$R_0$ (incumbent continues unmodified), $R_A$ ($\pi_A$ takes over the intervened agent(s) for the
remainder of the episode), and $R_B$ ($\pi_B$ takes over identically). For a unit $x=(s_t,e)$
over $M$ matched continuations,
\[
\hat{A}_A(x)=\frac{1}{M}\sum_{j=1}^{M}\left(R_A^j-R_0^j\right),
\qquad
\hat{A}_B(x)=\frac{1}{M}\sum_{j=1}^{M}\left(R_B^j-R_0^j\right),
\]
where $R$ is the binary episode win indicator, matching the crossover convention. Because $r_j$
is shared across arms, $\hat{A}_A$ and $\hat{A}_B$ isolate the effect of \emph{which policy acts
from this state onward} rather than continuation randomness.

\subsubsection{Routing Rule}
Each unit is routed to whichever teacher shows a strictly positive advantage over both zero and
the competing teacher:
\[
t^*(x)=
\begin{cases}
\pi_A, & \hat{A}_A(x)>\max\left(0,\hat{A}_B(x)\right),\\[3pt]
\pi_B, & \hat{A}_B(x)>\max\left(0,\hat{A}_A(x)\right),\\[3pt]
\mathrm{none}, & \max\left(\hat{A}_A(x),\hat{A}_B(x)\right)\le 0,\\[3pt]
\mathrm{none}, & \hat{A}_A(x)=\hat{A}_B(x)>0,
\end{cases}
\qquad
w(x)=
\begin{cases}
\hat{A}_{t^*}(x), & t^*(x)\neq\mathrm{none},\\[3pt]
0, & \text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]
A unit with no measured causal benefit, or an exact tie, receives zero weight rather than an
arbitrary assignment: a tie carries no evidence favoring either teacher. At states where both
agents are free, a usable joint intervention supersedes its constituent per-agent units, so no
state is double-counted.

\subsubsection{Anti-Trajectory-Shopping}
The $(o,a)$ trajectory used for supervision at a routed unit is obtained by rolling the
already-selected teacher $t^*(x)$ \emph{exactly once}, under a canonical seed derived
independently of every measurement seed $r_j$. Teacher selection and weight come exclusively from
the $M$-arm measurement; the canonical rollout's own outcome is never consulted and cannot
retroactively alter $t^*(x)$ or $w(x)$. This prevents implicitly selecting a favorable training
trajectory from among continuations whose outcomes were already observed.

\subsubsection{Causal Objective}
The auxiliary policy loss is a weighted negative log-likelihood of the routed teacher's action,
restricted to decision-eligible agents:
\[
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{causal}}
=
\frac{\sum_{t,i} d_t^i\,w_{t,i}\,\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{teacher}}(s_t,i,z)}
{\sum_{t,i} d_t^i\,w_{t,i}}.
\]
A committed agent ($d_t^i=0$) contributes to neither numerator nor denominator, so committed
frames cannot dilute effective supervision strength; a zero-weight unit contributes no gradient
by construction rather than by post-hoc filtering. The term is added to the task objective as
$\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{PPO}}+\lambda_{\mathrm{causal}}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{causal}}$
with $\lambda_{\mathrm{causal}}=0.05$, and never modifies reward, returns, GAE, or value targets.

\subsubsection{Outcome and Successor Repairs}
Causal commitment supervision did not clear the crossover gate. Neither did a sequence of
successor repairs applied to the same compressed incumbent, each preregistered and each varying
one mechanism: retention against an exponential-moving-average teacher; retention against a
frozen specialized reference; freezing the shared trunk so that only private per-latent
parameters adapt; and balanced sampling of the causal bank, which corrects a documented $3.5\times$
imbalance in behavior-changing supervision between latents. Two further candidates---a
Jensen--Shannon separation hinge and a second balanced-sampling evaluation---were held or aborted
without verdict rather than being reported as failures.

The repairs share a common presupposition that a provenance audit later invalidated: the
compressed incumbent was never produced by compressing $\pi_A$ and $\pi_B$ at all. It is a
fresh random-initialization run trained by task PPO with a latent slot, in which neither
specialist ever appeared. The repair sequence was therefore restoring specialization that had
never been installed.

\subsection{Teacher-Guided Compression}

We therefore compress the specialists directly. A fresh latent student, randomly initialized
with the incumbent's exact architecture, is trained by a purely supervised objective with no
reinforcement learning of any kind:
\[
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{distill}}
=
\tfrac{1}{2}D_{\mathrm{KL}}\!\left(\pi_A(\cdot\mid s)\,\|\,\pi_\theta(\cdot\mid s,z_0)\right)
+
\tfrac{1}{2}D_{\mathrm{KL}}\!\left(\pi_B(\cdot\mid s)\,\|\,\pi_\theta(\cdot\mid s,z_1)\right),
\]
evaluated per action head over masked distributions and averaged over decision-boundary heads
only. Both teachers are queried on the \emph{same} states, so the difference between $z_0$ and
$z_1$ is teacher strategy rather than state distribution. Only actor parameters are optimized;
critics are excluded and verified immobile. Training runs a fixed number of epochs with no early
stopping and no checkpoint selection, and the terminal weights are the only artifact.

Crossover is then measured \emph{before any reinforcement learning touches the student}, which
isolates compression from subsequent RL drift. The distinction is enforced structurally: no
training entry point in the codebase can load the distilled checkpoint.

The student reproduces its teachers closely (holdout argmax agreement $0.965$ for $z_0\!\sim\!\pi_A$
and $0.984$ for $z_1\!\sim\!\pi_B$; student $z_0/z_1$ Jensen--Shannon divergence $0.183$, marginally
\emph{above} the teachers' own $0.179$, so the branches are if anything more separated than the
strategies they imitate) yet does not clear the gate:
\[
\Delta_A=+0.094\;[-0.078,+0.266],
\qquad
\Delta_B=+0.141\;[-0.047,+0.313].
\]
Both point estimates are correctly signed; neither confidence interval clears zero. High-fidelity
imitation of both specialists is not sufficient for payoff-level crossover once the student drives
its own closed-loop state distribution.

\subsection{Mechanism Diagnostics}

Two candidate explanations were tested and, in the tested regime, rejected. Each was a
measurement with a preregistered falsification condition, not a new training method.

\paragraph{Covariate shift.}
If compounding imitation error drove the loss, teacher agreement measured on states the
\emph{student} visits should fall below agreement on teacher-visited states. It does not: on
$24{,}693$ student-generated states the distilled student agrees with its teachers at $0.971$ and
$0.986$, marginally \emph{above} its $0.965$/$0.984$ on teacher-visited holdout states. The
preregistered falsification condition was met and the DAgger-style successor was held after one
round rather than run to completion. The refutation is scoped to the CPU-generated deployment
distribution, since the sealed evaluation runs on a different device and trajectories are
device-dependent.

\paragraph{Decision leverage.}
If a small number of high-leverage mistakes drove the loss, substituting the teacher's action at
disagreement states should change outcomes more than perturbing a typical decision. It does not.
Using matched counterfactual branching---replaying a recorded prefix under assertion, seeding the
environment RNG identically across branches, and altering exactly one decision---the well-powered
$z_0$ arm shows $18$ of $20$ teacher substitutions producing an \emph{identical} final outcome,
with mean $|L_{\mathrm{margin}}|=0.100$ against a decision-sensitivity reference of $0.450$. The
$z_1$ arm was underpowered by construction ($6$ branch points) and no conclusion is drawn for it.
This underpowered result is not converted into evidence against the hypothesis and is not repaired
post hoc by changing the macro-level definition.

\paragraph{Where disagreement actually lives.}
A separate diagnostic, specified after the macro-level limitation was identified, analyzes the
already-recorded action vectors by head. It does \emph{not} retroactively redefine the DLD
macro-level hypothesis. Instead, it asks a new question: whether residual disagreement is
concentrated below the macro choice. The analysis reveals that macro-level agreement understates
disagreement substantially. Against the latent-matched teacher, macro disagreement is $1.11\%$
for $z_0$ and $0.23\%$ for $z_1$, while \emph{target} disagreement is $5.21\%$ and $1.60\%$
respectively---a factor of $4.7$ to $7.0$. The student agrees on \emph{what} to do far more
reliably than on \emph{which target}, and imitation fidelity is essentially pole-independent.

\subsection{Matched-Seed Evaluation and Instrument Calibration}
\label{sec:matched}

A control experiment revealed that the evaluation instrument itself carries substantial variance.
Evaluating the \emph{identical, bit-exact} specialists on two independent $64$-seed blocks
produced $\Delta_A=+0.125\;[-0.047,+0.297]$ on one and $\Delta_A=+0.453\;[+0.297,+0.609]$ on the
other---a swing of $0.33$ attributable entirely to which episodes were drawn, since the policies
cannot change. Pooled over both blocks ($n=128$) the reference is
$\Delta_A=+0.289\;[+0.164,+0.406]$ and $\Delta_B=+0.258\;[+0.141,+0.375]$.

\paragraph{Chronology of the calibration change.}
The matched-seed protocol was introduced \emph{after} the two Rung~0 blocks revealed substantial
block-to-block variation and therefore should be read as a post-calibration design correction,
not as part of the original $64$-seed gate. The chronology is fixed: Rung~0 was evaluated on the
two independent calibration blocks; once their variance was observed, the union of their $128$
seeds and every per-seed Rung~0 outcome were frozen; Rung~0 was never rerun or reselected
afterward. All later ladder rungs were specified prospectively to use that same frozen set. The
calibration change is therefore used only for subsequent architecture comparisons and does not
retroactively reclassify earlier sealed results.

Consequently, comparing an architecture's $\Delta$ on its own fresh block against a reference
$\Delta$ on a different block compares two noisy averages. We therefore fix a
\textbf{matched-seed protocol}: every architecture is evaluated on the same $128$-seed set for
which the reference's per-seed outcomes are frozen, and the primary statistic is the
\emph{within-seed} difference
\[
d(s)=\Delta_{R_k}(s)-\Delta_{R_0}(s),
\qquad
D=\mathbb{E}_s\!\left[d(s)\right],
\]
under a paired bootstrap, with ``sharing damaged specialization'' declared on a pole only if
$\mathrm{UCB}_{95}(D)<0$. This cancels per-seed difficulty: both architectures face the identical
episode and only the architecture differs. The reference is never re-run.

\subsection{The Parameter-Sharing Ladder}

Every mechanism examined above operates on \emph{training} while holding the architecture fixed.
Representation sharing is the one axis never varied. We therefore reframe the question from
``which regularizer restores crossover?'' to
\[
\boxed{\text{How much parameter sharing can a latent policy tolerate before complementary
strategies stop surviving?}}
\]
a question with an informative answer in either direction.

The ladder holds the distillation recipe, dataset, schedule, and evaluation seeds fixed and
varies only which modules are tied between the two branches. Each rung uses two complete actors
of the \emph{specialists'} own architecture, so a loss can never be attributed to reduced
per-strategy capacity; tying is by module identity, verified both by object identity and by
unique-parameter arithmetic. A mixed-$z$ minibatch gives each shared module gradient from both
strategies in the same step, which is precisely the phenomenon under test.

Rungs~2 and~3 differ in exactly one place, which the cumulative column makes explicit: Rung~2

\paragraph{Rung 0 as a structural positive control.}
Rung~0 installs $\pi_A$ and $\pi_B$ verbatim behind a $z$-dispatch wrapper with zero shared
parameters, verified bit-exact against both specialists on the evaluation device (maximum
absolute logit difference $0$, argmax identical on all four heads, macro and target checked
separately). It is not compression; its role is to establish that the dispatch-and-evaluate path
can express crossover at all. Its failure would have indicted the instrument rather than any
architecture, and it is sealed first for that reason.

On its first sealed $64$-seed block the gate passed on Pole~B ($+0.188\;[+0.031,+0.344]$) but not
on Pole~A ($+0.125\;[-0.047,+0.297]$). Because Rung~0 \emph{is} the frozen specialists, this
cannot reflect any change in the policies, and an independent second block returned
$+0.453\;[+0.297,+0.609]$ on the same pole. The crossover \emph{direction} was positive on both
poles in both blocks; the magnitude was not stable. We therefore read Rung~0 as establishing that
the dispatch path expresses crossover in the correct direction on both poles, and we treat the
block-to-block magnitude discrepancy not as a property of any architecture but as the variance of
the evaluation instrument---which is precisely what motivates the matched-seed protocol of
Section~\ref{sec:matched}. The reading rule---direction decides, while magnitude noise is handled
by the matched design rather than by demanding block-to-block agreement---was frozen while the
second block was still running, before either of its Pole-B cells or any confidence interval
existed.

\paragraph{Rung 1.}
Sharing only the perception encoder preserves crossover: both poles clear the gate independently,
and neither within-seed difference against Rung~0 reaches significance. Decomposing the change by
role is more informative than the aggregate. On-pole cells (each latent on its own pole) shift by
$-0.012$ on average, while off-pole cells shift by $+0.047$, driven by $z_0$ on Pole~B rising
$+0.086$. The specialists are not degraded; the \emph{inappropriate} strategy improves, narrowing
$\Delta$ from below. We name this mechanism \textbf{competence leakage}---shared parameters
transfer general task skill between strategies---and distinguish it from destructive interference,
in which the on-pole specialist itself would decay.

\paragraph{Preregistered prediction for Rung 2.}
Before Rung~2 was trained we froze the prediction that, if competence leakage is the operative
mechanism, additional sharing will reduce crossover \emph{primarily through increased off-pole
performance while preserving on-pole performance}; a substantial on-pole decline would instead
support destructive interference. The evaluator computes and classifies this decomposition as a
primary reported quantity, so the mechanism test cannot become a post-hoc reading. Pole~B is the
diagnostic pole: Rung~1's $\mathrm{UCB}_{95}(D_B)=+0.023$ leaves it far closer to registering a
detectable loss than Pole~A's $+0.102$.

\paragraph{Fidelity confound and interpretation guardrail.}
Because a more heavily shared student may simply fit its teachers less well, we further froze a
comparability band---final holdout agreement within $0.010$ of Rung~1's $0.967/0.989$---anchored
on the only two fully converged distilled students available beforehand, which differ by at most
$0.005$. The band is an \emph{interpretation guardrail}, not a theoretically privileged threshold
and not a success gate. We therefore report the continuous agreement and KL values for every rung
regardless of which side of the band it falls on, and we do not infer a discontinuity at the
boundary. If a rung falls outside the band, any crossover degradation is reported as
\emph{sharing-associated} but not uniquely attributable to leakage or interference, preserving
the distinction between ``sharing causes poorer fit'' and ``sharing destroys crossover despite
equally good fit.''

Rung~2 met the band. Its terminal holdout agreement is $0.963/0.985$, against Rung~1's
$0.967/0.989$ and the offline distilled student's $0.965/0.984$---inside the band by $0.006$ on
both latents, and reported here side by side as the amendment requires irrespective of outcome.
Rung~2 therefore achieved teacher-fit parity with Rung~1 within the preregistered comparability
band, which eliminates fidelity as a competing explanation and licenses a strong mechanism-level
interpretation of any matched-seed crossover change it exhibits. We note descriptively that
Rung~2's branch separation is the lowest of the three distilled students (Jensen--Shannon $0.172$,
against $0.178$ for Rung~1, $0.183$ for the offline student, and $0.179$ for the teachers
themselves); it is the first student whose branches are measurably less separated than the
strategies it imitates, which is the direction additional sharing would predict. This quantity is
not gated and is not used as evidence on its own.

\paragraph{Rung~3 and training-route attribution.}
The sharing ladder isolates parameter sharing only \emph{within the fixed pure-distillation
recipe}. Rung~3 may match the module-sharing pattern of the historical compressed architecture,
but the two models differ in training route: Rung~3 is trained by direct specialist distillation,
whereas the historical incumbent was trained from random initialization by task PPO and never
received the specialists directly. Therefore a Rung~3 PASS would establish that the architecture
\emph{can} preserve crossover under direct distillation; it would not show that sharing was
irrelevant to the historical PPO failure. Conversely, a Rung~3 FAIL would be evidence about the
distilled sharing ladder, not a clean replication of the PPO failure. Any training-route claim
must be tested or discussed separately from the sharing ablation.

\subsection{Claim Hierarchy and External-Validity Follow-Up}

The evidence is interpreted in a fixed hierarchy. First, strategic demand and separate-specialist
crossover establish that complementary policies exist. Second, direct compression asks whether
those policies can coexist in one latent-conditioned policy. Third, mechanism diagnostics test
specific explanations for any compression shortfall. Fourth, the calibrated sharing ladder varies
representation sharing while holding training and evaluation fixed. Only after the nominal
mechanism is localized do we ask whether the resulting conclusion transfers beyond the base
setting.

Accordingly, the strongest claim supported by the present methodology is intentionally narrow:
\emph{within the nominal $2$-vs-$2$ two-pole maritime task, under the frozen distillation recipe
and matched-seed CUDA evaluation, we can localize whether and how parameter sharing changes
payoff-level crossover}. Broader claims about multi-agent reinforcement learning require a
separate external-validity stage. The planned confirmatory extensions are larger team sizes
($4$-vs-$4$ and $6$-vs-$6$) and deployment perturbations in localization, motion, and control
delay. Those experiments are not used for method selection and, until completed, are not cited as
evidence for generality.
