\subsection{Specialization Across Team Sizes}

The Experimental Evaluation reports one method at three team sizes:
\[
\text{same symmetric dual-branch method}\ \rightarrow\ 2\mathrm{v}2,\;4\mathrm{v}4,\;6\mathrm{v}6.
\]
Poles~A and~B are treated symmetrically (same recipe, budget, role rule
\(k=\lceil N/3\rceil\) with \(k=1,2,2\) at \(N=2,4,6\), and the same
crossover test). Within each pole, ATTACK and DEFEND are jointly trained in
one shared 200\,k rollout: both branches receive PPO; DEFEND alone has an
extra temporary supervised scaffold. Leaving ATTACK frozen is an ablation,
not this method. No candidate selection, no alternative role menu, and no
historical asymmetric rescue enters this claim.

For each team size we report \(\Delta_A\) and \(\Delta_B\), the mean
advantage of each strategy on its own regime. Positive values on
\emph{both} poles are required for two-sided complementarity. Tables use
mean \(\pm\) sample standard deviation of per-seed paired win differences
(\(\{-1,0,+1\}\)); bootstrap confidence intervals are stated in the text.
All three rows are the sealed dual-branch matched-128 evaluations
(\(n=128\)).

Under this method, 2v2 satisfies the two-sided criterion:
\[
\Delta_A=+0.156,\quad 95\%\ \mathrm{CI}=[+0.047,+0.266],
\qquad
\Delta_B=+0.422,\quad 95\%\ \mathrm{CI}=[+0.336,+0.508].
\]
At 4v4 the method retains an A-side advantage but fails on Pole~B:
\[
\Delta_A=+0.195,\quad 95\%\ \mathrm{CI}=[+0.070,+0.320],
\qquad
\Delta_B=-0.086,\quad 95\%\ \mathrm{CI}=[-0.148,-0.031].
\]
At 6v6 the pattern is inverted: Pole~B is strongly specialized while
Pole~A is not established,
\[
\Delta_A=-0.062,\quad 95\%\ \mathrm{CI}=[-0.148,+0.023],
\qquad
\Delta_B=+0.578,\quad 95\%\ \mathrm{CI}=[+0.484,+0.672].
\]
Thus only 2v2 meets the complementarity criterion under the frozen
symmetric dual-branch methodology. We interpret the 4v4 and 6v6 outcomes
as empirical limitations of that method at those scales, not as evidence
from a different recipe or from opportunistic reselection.

Score-margin differences on the same matched-128 seeds tell the same
directional story (2v2: both poles positive in mean; 4v4: \(\Delta_B\)
negative; 6v6: \(\Delta_A\) negative with interval below zero on margin).
We do not use an alternative methodology to replace any of these cells.


\subsection{Baseline Comparison}
\label{sec:baselines-provenance}

The remainder of this section records older baseline and noise comparisons
that do not use the symmetric dual-branch method above. They are
\emph{not} evidence for that method and should not be read as replacing
Table~\ref{tab:main_specialization}.

We next summarize those historical comparisons against alternative policy
organizations. The specialist baseline executes the repaired Pole-A and
Pole-B policies directly, without the explicit heuristic ATTACK/DEFEND
decomposition. The Generalist, Share-Encoder, and Fully Shared+$z$ arms are
members of one distillation family: the same teachers $\pi_A,\pi_B$ and the
same suite dataset, differing in architecture and whether a strategy label
$z$ is provided. The Generalist is the no-$z$ ablation in that family, so
its comparison to Fully Shared+$z$ isolates strategy conditioning; it is
reported with absolute win rates rather than $\Delta_A/\Delta_B$ because a
single unconditioned policy has no crossover specialization quantity.

Table~\ref{tab:role_baseline_comparison} first isolates the effect of explicit
role allocation under those historical constructions. For 2v2, the
role-allocation row is the paired diagnostic evaluated on the same seeds as
the corresponding no-role specialist baseline. For 4v4, the available
no-role and role-allocated results come from separate sealed evaluations
and should not be interpreted as a seed-by-seed paired comparison. For 6v6,
the no-role baseline and the role-allocated evaluation share the same 128
seeds, so that historical comparison is paired.

At 2v2, the repaired specialists without explicit heuristic roles do
not exhibit reciprocal specialization because the Pole-B effect is negative.
Introducing the ATTACK/DEFEND decomposition changes the Pole-B specialization
effect to the intended positive direction while retaining a positive Pole-A
effect.

The paired 2v2 diagnostic shows this most directly:
\(\Delta_B\) changes from \(-0.055\) without heuristic roles to \(+0.523\)
with heuristic roles. In the same diagnostic, the Pole-A policy's win rate on
Pole B decreases from \(0.648\) without the explicit role decomposition to
\(0.070\) after ATTACK/DEFEND roles are introduced. Thus, the role allocation
increases strategic separation primarily by constraining the Pole-A strategy
on the opponent for which it is not intended.

At 4v4, the historical asymmetric role-allocated construction in
Table~\ref{tab:role_baseline_comparison} shows a large positive
\(\Delta_B=+0.406\), but that row is outside the symmetric dual-branch
method of Table~\ref{tab:main_specialization}. We keep it as historical
provenance only and do not treat it as evidence for the method.

At 6v6, where the comparison is paired on the same 128 seeds, the role
allocation raises $\Delta_B$ from $+0.031$ to $+0.117$, a paired change of
$+0.086$ (95\% interval $[+0.023, +0.148]$), and changes $\Delta_A$ by
$-0.031$ ($[-0.078, +0.016]$). The mechanism is the same as at the smaller
team sizes: with a committed defender, the Pole-A strategy wins less often on
Pole~B ($0.945 \to 0.859$), while the Pole-B specialist is unchanged
($0.977$).

At 2v2, both distilled students retain positive mean specialization, but the
effects are much smaller than for the dual-branch teachers in
Table~\ref{tab:main_specialization} ($\Delta_A=+0.156$, $\Delta_B=+0.422$).
Share-Encoder reaches $\Delta_A=+0.125$ and $\Delta_B=+0.070$; Fully Shared+$z$
reaches $\Delta_A=+0.078$ and $\Delta_B=+0.062$. Relative to those teachers,
the main loss is on Pole~B: sharing preserves a weak intended-direction
signal, but does not recover the large $\Delta_B$ of the dual-branch system.
(Historical asymmetric role-allocated rows elsewhere in this section remain
provenance only and are not the method claim.)

At 4v4 the distilled students reproduce their teachers, the no-role specialists
($\Delta_A=+0.273$, $\Delta_B=-0.156$): Share-Encoder reaches $+0.281$ /
$-0.156$ and Fully Shared+$z$ $+0.367$ / $-0.109$. As with the teachers, the
strategy distilled from the Pole-A specialist wins more often on both poles
($0.805$ and $0.680$ for Share-Encoder), so $\Delta_B$ is negative. These
historical sharing rows therefore show how sharing transforms an incomplete
teacher distinction; they should not be read as evidence that sharing
preserved a complementary 4v4 pair
(Table~\ref{tab:main_specialization}). The historical asymmetric
role-allocated $\Delta_B=+0.406$ remains provenance only.

At 6v6 all policies win most games (0.86--0.98), and the distilled students
show small effects in the intended direction (Share-Encoder $+0.039$ /
$+0.039$, Fully Shared+$z$ $+0.031$ / $+0.008$), close to their no-role
teachers ($+0.094$ / $+0.031$), with the highest held-out agreement with the
teachers of any team size (0.99 / 0.94). As at 2v2 and 4v4, none of the
parameter-sharing baselines reaches the Pole-B effect of the role-allocated
system ($+0.117$).
Under the dual-branch teachers of Table~\ref{tab:main_specialization},
unselected matched-128 evaluation already fails to establish Pole~A
($\Delta_A=-0.062$, CI $[-0.148,+0.023]$) while Pole~B is strong. A Stage-4
student that likewise lacks A-side crossover therefore does
\emph{not} demonstrate that compression destroyed an established A-side
distinction; positive teacher $\Delta_A$ on a post-hoc top-50 subset remains
selected and diagnostic only.

\subsection{Robustness to Deployment Noise}

control delay independently at 2v2, 4v4, and 6v6. Training is unchanged; all
perturbations are introduced only during evaluation.

\(\Delta_A\) and \(\Delta_B\). The nominal results in
Table~\ref{tab:main_specialization} provide the corresponding unperturbed
reference. Matched-seed degradation relative to a same-block nominal run will
be reported where available (the 2v2 noise suite uses one matched seed block

\subsection{What the experiments show}
\label{sec:exp-takeaway}

We evaluate whether opponent-conditioned specialization survives policy
sharing under a common role organization. The evaluated teachers establish
two-sided specialization at 2v2
($\Delta_A=+0.156$, $\Delta_B=+0.422$; both 95\% intervals above zero), but
exhibit different one-sided limitations at larger team sizes: 4v4 keeps an
A-side advantage while reversing Pole~B, and 6v6 keeps a strong B-side
advantage while failing to establish Pole~A on the unselected matched-128
block. Distillation substantially reduces actor parameters
(about $14\,\mathrm{M}\to 3.5\,\mathrm{M}$), while closed-loop preservation
of two-sided specialization remains incompletely validated: Stage-4 student
crossovers that clear both poles are presently available on post-hoc
selected OWN50/TOP50 subsets, not on an unselected seed block scored jointly
with the teachers.

\paragraph{Limits (stated once).}
Selected top-50 views are descriptive. Ordinary bootstrap intervals on a
post-hoc ranked subset do not account for the selection procedure and must
not be read as confirmatory support. Training variability and one-seed
teacher draws further bound the claim. The decisive next measurement is
unselected evaluation of the compressed 2v2 students on the same matched
seed block as the teachers; if both crossover advantages survive there,
4v4 and 6v6 become carefully bounded failure cases rather than the center of
the contribution.
