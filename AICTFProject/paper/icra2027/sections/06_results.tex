\subsection{Evaluation data and plot map}
\label{sec:results-data}

All headline numbers below come from \emph{sealed} evaluation artifacts with
fixed seed blocks and terminal checkpoints (no post-hoc checkpoint shopping).
Bootstrap intervals use a paired percentile procedure over seeds
($n_{\mathrm{boot}}=20{,}000$, $\alpha=0.05$) unless a caption states otherwise.

\paragraph{Data blocks.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Strategic demand (V3).}
  Paired confirmation on $n{=}192$ seeds
  ($6000001$--$6000192$): conflicting best-response payoff gaps and concealment
  gates on both regimes.
  \item \textbf{Independent experts and generalist.}
  Held-out $n{=}64$ evaluations of $\pi_A$, $\pi_B$, and $\pi_G$.
  \item \textbf{Progressive sharing (primary Claim~A).}
  Matched $n{=}128$ CUDA evaluations of Share-0 / Encoder / Backbone / Macro
  under forced $z_0,z_1$ on both regimes (identical seeds across rungs).
  \item \textbf{Matched qualitative.}
  Single sealed seed $11960003$ under Share-Encoder: trajectory overlay and
  frame grid with CUDA terminal-score fidelity checks against the sealed CSV.
  \item \textbf{Role telemetry (exploratory).}
  Episode-mean defender/attacker proxies, $n{=}24$/cell; \emph{not} a
  specialization gate.
  \item \textbf{Robustness.}
  Matched $n{=}128$ dose--response under localization, motion, and latency
  perturbations (external validity).
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{How figures answer questions.}
Demand and specialist $\Delta$ appear as sealed numerical contrasts; absolute
win rates as Fig.~\ref{fig:winrate-context}; Claim~A causal chain as
Fig.~\ref{fig:claim-a}; sharing erosion as Fig.~\ref{fig:sharing-ladder} and
Table~\ref{tab:sharing-results}; imitation$\neq$payoff as
Fig.~\ref{fig:hierarchy}; recognizable strategies as
Figs.~\ref{fig:traj}--\ref{fig:roles-ext}; robustness as Fig.~\ref{fig:robust}.

\paragraph{How to read the plots (terminology).}
Throughout Results and figure captions we use a small set of pre-specified
statistical phrases. They are \emph{not} informal judgments about whether a run
``looked good.''

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Specialization / crossover.}
  Strategy codes $z_0$ and $z_1$ are payoff-specialized only if each code
  yields a higher win rate than the other against its intended opponent regime.
  Formally,
  $\Delta_A=V(z_0,A)-V(z_1,A)$ and $\Delta_B=V(z_1,B)-V(z_0,B)$ must both be
  strictly positive with $95\%$ lower confidence bounds
  $\mathrm{LCB}_{95}(\Delta)>0$ under a paired seed bootstrap
  ($n_{\mathrm{boot}}=20{,}000$, $\alpha=0.05$). We call this the
  \emph{specialization criterion} (equivalently, specialization crossover).

  \item \textbf{Satisfies criterion / fails criterion}
  (figures may label these \textsc{pass}/\textsc{fail}).
  The pre-specified specialization criterion above is met, or is not.
  Point estimates alone never decide the label.

  \item \textbf{$\mathrm{LCB}_{95}$ / $\mathrm{UCB}_{95}$.}
  Lower/upper endpoints of a percentile bootstrap $95\%$ confidence interval
  over matched evaluation seeds.

  \item \textbf{Detectable loss.}
  Relative to the Share-0 expert-dispatch reference, a paired change
  $D_p=\Delta_p^{(\mathrm{cond})}-\Delta_p^{(\mathrm{Share\textrm{-}0})}$ is a
  \emph{detectable specialization loss} on regime $p$ only if
  $\mathrm{UCB}_{95}(D_p)<0$ (the entire CI lies below zero).

  \item \textbf{Contested / unsaturated matchup}
  (when used for opponent-regime demand).
  Neither side of a policy--opponent pairing is effectively saturated: win rates
  are not near $0$ or $1$, so meaningful performance variation remains and
  specialization contrasts are informative rather than floor/ceiling artifacts.

  \item \textbf{Certified} (opponent-regime demand only).
  A preregistered strategic-demand protocol for a scale/regime pair was
  satisfied under its sealed evaluation; it does \emph{not} by itself imply
  that a learned controller specializes.
\end{itemize}

\noindent
Figures also report means with $95\%$ CIs; sample sizes are stated in captions.

\subsection{Do the opponent regimes require different strategies?}

A preregistered opponent-conditioned demand protocol (V3; $n{=}192$ paired seeds)
establishes that Regimes~A and~B admit \emph{conflicting} best responses with
concealed strategic context on both sides. Payoff gaps between scripted
guard-style and breach-style responses satisfy the sealed floors
\[
\Delta_G(A)=+0.297\;[+0.203,+0.391],
\qquad
\Delta_B(B)=+0.453\;[+0.385,+0.526],
\]
with concealment gates $\mathrm{LCB}_{95}(p_C)>0.50$ on both regimes
($p_C(A)=0.943$, $p_C(B)=0.917$). Matchups remain contested rather than
floor/ceiling-dominated. This certifies strategic \emph{demand} in the
environment; it does not yet show that a learned controller discovers two modes.

\subsection{Can independent expert policies specialize?}

Independent experts $\pi_A$ and $\pi_B$, scored under a preregistered protocol
on a held-out $64$-seed block, satisfy the specialization criterion:
\[
\Delta_A = +0.172\;[+0.016,+0.328],
\qquad
\Delta_B = +0.297\;[+0.125,+0.469].
\]
The generalist attains
$V(\pi_G,A)=0.375\;[0.265,0.500]$ and
$V(\pi_G,B)=0.500\;[0.375,0.625]$.

On the matched $128$-seed Share-0 expert-dispatch reference (bit-exact $z$
selection of the verbatim experts; not a distilled model),
\[
\Delta_A = +0.289\;[+0.164,+0.406],
\qquad
\Delta_B = +0.258\;[+0.141,+0.375],
\]
so the evaluation path can express specialization when nothing is shared.

\subsection{Can distilled strategy modes preserve specialization under sharing?}

\paragraph{Shared perception only (Share-Encoder).}
Under the common ladder distillation recipe with full per-strategy branch
capacity and only the CNN encoder tied, Share-Encoder satisfies the
specialization criterion on the matched $128$-seed set:
\[
\Delta_A=+0.266\;[+0.148,+0.383],
\qquad
\Delta_B=+0.164\;[+0.047,+0.281].
\]
Holdout argmax agreements are $0.967/0.989$; branch Jensen--Shannon divergence
is $0.178$ (experts $0.179$). Paired contrasts vs.\ Share-0 show no detectable
loss ($D_A=-0.023$, $D_B=-0.094$; both $\mathrm{UCB}_{95}\ge 0$). Thus
matched-state distillation into discrete $z$ modes preserves closed-loop
specialization when only perception is shared
(Fig.~\ref{fig:claim-a}, panel~b; Fig.~\ref{fig:sharing-ladder}).

\paragraph{Absolute performance context.}
In addition to the paired specialization contrasts, we report regime-specific
win rates for the mixed-opponent generalist, independently trained experts, and
the preserved Share-Encoder policy (Fig.~\ref{fig:winrate-context}).
These values provide absolute-performance context and are \emph{not} used as the
criterion for strategy specialization.
Independent expert and generalist results use their held-out $64$-seed
evaluations, whereas shared-policy results use the matched $128$-seed evaluation
block.

\paragraph{Prior compression ablation (not Claim~A).}
An earlier offline student under a heavily shared/compressed architecture
($n{=}64$) imitated experts well (agreement $0.965/0.984$, JS $0.183$) yet
\emph{failed} the specialization criterion
($\Delta_A=+0.094\,[-0.078,+0.266]$, $\Delta_B=+0.141\,[-0.047,+0.313]$).
That artifact is architecturally closest to Share-Macro, not Share-Encoder. It
shows that high action-level imitation can coexist with failed closed-loop
specialization under aggressive sharing---reinforcing the measurement hierarchy
(Fig.~\ref{fig:hierarchy})---and must not be read as ``distillation failed before
sharing.''

\noindent
Most of the parameter saving appears at Share-Encoder ($\approx\!48\%$ fewer
unique actor parameters than the two-expert deployment) while the specialization
criterion is preserved.
Share-Backbone and Share-Macro add only about $1.5$ percentage points of further
reduction, yet paired evaluation already detects Regime-$A$ loss and Share-Macro
fails the absolute criterion---so aggressive sharing buys little footprint and

\paragraph{Share-Encoder.}
Share-Encoder holdout argmax agreements are $0.967/0.989$; these values define
the imitation-fidelity reference band
(Section~\ref{sec:fidelity}).
Sharing only the perception encoder preserves the specialization criterion on
both regimes, with no detectable paired loss vs.\ Share-0.
Decomposing by role: on-regime cells shift by $-0.012$ on average, while
cross-regime cells shift by $+0.047$, driven by $z_0$ under Regime~B rising
$+0.086$---consistent with mild strategy homogenization rather than on-regime
collapse.

\paragraph{Share-Backbone.}
Share-Backbone terminal holdout agreement is $0.963/0.985$ (within $0.010$ of
Share-Encoder on both codes), so fidelity is comparable.
Branch Jensen--Shannon divergence is $0.172$ (Share-Encoder $0.178$; experts
$0.179$). The specialization criterion still holds on both regimes, but the
paired contrast against Share-0 shows a detectable reduction on Regime~A
($\mathrm{UCB}_{95}(D_A)<0$). On-regime performance declines on average while
cross-regime performance rises---consistent with specialization loss / negative
transfer under fidelity-comparable backbone sharing.

\paragraph{Share-Macro.}
Share-Macro terminal holdout agreement is $0.936/0.969$, outside the
imitation-fidelity band anchored on Share-Encoder. The specialization criterion
is not satisfied ($\mathrm{LCB}_{95}(\Delta_A)=0$). Paired evaluation likewise
shows a detectable reduction on Regime~A. Because imitation fidelity is not
comparable, we report this as sharing-associated and fit-confounded degradation
and do not uniquely attribute it to homogenization vs.\ capacity loss.

recognizable team strategies: \emph{qualitative trajectories}
(Figs.~\ref{fig:traj}--\ref{fig:qual}), \emph{role-allocation proxies}

\subsection{Attempted strategy-preserving fine-tuning (negative result)}

Counterfactual Strategy Correction (CSC) and Strategy-Preserving Fine-Tuning
(SPFT) do not recover the specialization criterion under frozen matched
protocols; complete formulation and numbers appear in the supplementary
material. These interventions are not credited for Share-Encoder satisfying the
criterion.
